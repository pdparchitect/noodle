# Games

Describe a game to a bot and play it minutes later, on the TV in your living
room, on your iPhone or iPad, or on your Mac. No store and nothing to install
for each game: it opens right from the conversation.

<img width="100%" alt="Three friends on a sofa playing a pixel platformer a bot made, on the living-room TV with game controllers." src="images/games-tv.png" />

- **On the TV.** Turn on Screen Mirroring on your iPhone and open the game. It
  fills the TV and your phone becomes the controller.
- **With a controller.** Pick up a PlayStation, Xbox or Switch controller and
  press a button. It plays the game straight away, on the phone, the TV or the
  Mac.
- **Together on the couch.** Ask for a two-player game and play it side by side
  on one TV or one Mac.
- **Together from anywhere.** Share the bot through a Noodle Hub and your
  friends join the same game from their own phones and Macs, or take turns in a
  game you all share.
- **Made to measure.** Don't like something? Say so while you play, and the bot
  changes it.

A game is a noodlet, one of the small apps your bots build with
[Noodle Applet](../Applet/README.md).

## On your Mac or on a Hub

Where the game lives depends on where its bot does.

- **A bot on your Mac** makes games in Noodle Applet on that Mac. You play them
  there, or on a TV the Mac plays on. To reach them from your iPhone or iPad, turn
  on This Mac as a Hub in **Settings → Hub**; it serves only your own devices.
- **A bot on a [Noodle Hub](../Hub/README.md)** makes games on the Hub. You and
  the people you share the conversation with open them from any Mac, iPhone or
  iPad joined to the Hub. Each game runs on the device that opens it, or live
  from the Hub, and keeps its saved data on the Hub. This is the way to play with
  other people.

## Make a game

For a bot on your Mac, install Noodle Applet first; a Hub has it already. Ask
the bot for the game you want: what it is about, how it plays, how it should
look. Ask for changes the same way, as you play it. Bots
can open the game, play it, take screenshots and record short videos of it with
sound, so they can check their own work and show you what changed.

Games made for a controller list the buttons they use. That is what lets you play
them with a game controller on a Mac, or with on-screen controls on iPhone and
iPad. If a game has no controls on the phone, ask the bot to add them.

## Play on a Mac

When a bot shares a game, it appears in the conversation as a card.

A game from a bot on your Mac opens in Noodle Applet when you click its card.
Every such game also appears in the Applet library, under **Games** in its
**Categories** section.

A game from a bot on a Noodle Hub opens in a panel in Noodle and runs on your
Mac. Choose **Run on Hub** in the panel to watch it live from the Hub instead.
Noodle remembers the choice for each game; right-click its card to choose before
it opens.

<img width="100%" alt="A Mini Games group in Noodle where a bot has shared Bubble Buddies, a retro game for one to four players, as cards in the conversation." src="images/games-mac.png" />

Either way:

- Sound plays only from the game in front.
- A game that turns with the mouse can take over the pointer; press Escape to
  get it back.
- A connected game controller plays the game in front. It presses the game's
  buttons, so nothing is drawn on the screen.

## Play on iPhone and iPad

Games reach the phone through a Noodle Hub, including This Mac as a Hub. Tap
the game's card in the conversation. It runs on the phone, or live from the Hub
if you choose **Run on Hub**. Games that list their buttons show on-screen
controls over the game; the controller button at the top hides and shows them,
and the keyboard button beside it still types. To feel the buttons as you
press them, turn on **Haptics for On-Screen Controls** in Settings.

<img width="300" alt="A castle-road game on iPhone with on-screen controls: a d-pad, a second pad and labelled buttons over the game." src="images/games-iphone.png" />

Pair a game controller with the phone and it plays the game too. Once you press
something on it, only the buttons it has no room for stay on the screen. While a
controller is connected, the phone does not dim or lock. Games make sound in
Silent Mode, as videos do.

Press the controller's **View** button (**Create** on PlayStation, **−** on
Switch) to see the conversation's other games. Move with the d-pad or stick,
press A to switch to one or choose **Close Game**. B or View again goes back to
the game.

## Play on a TV

**From iPhone or iPad** (iOS 27): turn on Screen Mirroring to an Apple TV or
AirPlay TV, or connect a TV with a cable, and open a game. It plays full screen on
the TV, and the phone turns sideways and becomes its controller. **Show on
iPhone** brings the game back to the phone without starting over, and **Show on
TV** sends it back. Closing the game returns the TV to mirroring. With a game
controller paired, the phone shows the controllers and their batteries instead.

**From a Mac**, for games from a bot on that Mac: first add the TV as a separate
display. In Noodle Applet, choose **File → Play On → Add TV or Display**, then
pick the TV from the add menu in Displays settings. With the game in front,
choose the TV from **File → Play On**, or right-click the game's card in the
library. **Bring Back to This Mac** ends it. A game made at a fixed size keeps
its shape on the TV, scaled up and centred on black. Keyboards, mice and
trackpads stay connected to the Mac.

## Play together

With a bot on your Mac, you can play side by side. To play with people
elsewhere, keep the bot on a Noodle Hub and share a group conversation with
them.

- **On the same screen, from anywhere** (Hub). Everyone opens the game and
  chooses **Run on Hub**. There is one game running on the Hub, everyone sees it
  live, and everyone's buttons reach it. It moves at the pace of the slowest
  connection.
- **Taking turns** (Hub). Each person runs the game on their own device, and the
  game keeps its saved data on the Hub, so everyone shares the same saves. Ask
  the bot for a game that saves each move and checks for the others', such as a
  board game or a shared world you build in turns.
- **Side by side** (Mac or Hub). Play on one Mac or one TV. Ask the bot for a
  game where two players share the keyboard. A game controller plays one player;
  a game can read further controllers on a Mac if you ask the bot for that, but
  not on iPhone or iPad.

Games that use the camera, microphone or devices on your local network always
run on each person's own device, so they cannot be watched live from the Hub.
